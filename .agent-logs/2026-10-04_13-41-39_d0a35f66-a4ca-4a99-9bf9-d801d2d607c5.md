**Bot account sign-in**

When the bot joins a Google Meet, it needs an identity. Meet treats anonymous guests as the most suspicious kind of joiner, and many hosts block them or make them wait. A signed-in Google account gets refused far less often.

So the bot has its own dedicated Google account, something like `milo.notetaker@gmail.com`. It is not your account and not any user's account.

- **Your passwords stay yours.** Milo never sees the bot account's password. You sign in once by hand through a remote-screen (VNC) window into the bot's container, and the logged-in browser session is saved in a Docker volume.
- **Name:** you set that account's display name to "Milo Notetaker", which is what meeting participants see.
- **One time:** after the sign-in, the bot reuses the saved session.
- **Optional in code:** the bot can also join as an anonymous guest by typing a name. Without the signed-in account, expect many meetings to refuse it.

The steps are in [docs/bot.md](docs/bot.md), section 1.

**One bot for everyone, or one per user?**

Today it's one shared bot identity for all users: the same Google account and the same "Milo Notetaker" name. I'd keep it that way. Per-user bots would mean every user creating and signing in their own Google account for Milo, which is a lot of friction and credential handling.
